Alice was sitting beside her sister on a warm afternoon when something very strange hurried past.
It was a White Rabbit.
That alone was unusual.
But the Rabbit was wearing a waistcoat.
And carrying a pocket watch.
“Oh dear! Oh dear! I shall be late!” he cried.
Alice jumped to her feet.
A talking rabbit with a watch was far too interesting to ignore.
She followed him across the grass and watched him disappear into a rabbit hole.
Alice went after him.
Down she fell.
And down.
And down.
The walls of the tunnel were lined with cupboards, maps, pictures, and shelves.
lice had plenty of time to look at them as she fell.

At last—

THUMP!

She landed safely on a pile of leaves.

The White Rabbit disappeared around a corner.

“Wait!”

Alice hurried after him and entered a long hall filled with doors.

Every one was locked.

Then Alice noticed a tiny golden key.

It opened the smallest door in the hall.

Beyond it was the most beautiful garden Alice had ever seen.

There was only one problem.

The door was much too small for her.

On a nearby table stood a bottle marked:

DRINK ME.

Alice took a careful sip.

At once, she began to shrink.

“That's better!”

Soon she was small enough for the door.

Then she remembered the key.

It was still on top of the table.

“Oh, honestly!”

Nearby, Alice found a little cake marked:

EAT ME.

She took a bite.

WHOOSH!

Alice grew so tall that her head nearly touched the ceiling.

“This is getting ridiculous!”

She tried one thing after another until she became small again.

By then, Alice had cried so much that her tears had made a pool across the floor.

She splashed into it with a Mouse, a Dodo, and several very wet birds.

“We must get dry!” cried the Dodo.

“How?” asked Alice.

“A race!”

“Where does it start?”

“Here!”

“Where does it finish?”

“Also here!”

Everyone began running in circles.

After a while, the Dodo raised one wing.

“The race is over!”

“Who won?” Alice asked.

“Everyone has won, and everyone must have prizes!”

Alice handed out the sweets from her pocket.

“And what do I get?”

The Dodo thought carefully.

Then he took Alice's own thimble from her hand and presented it to her.

“This.”

Alice stared at it.

“Thank you… I think.”

After leaving the strange race, Alice wandered into the woods.

There she found a blue Caterpillar sitting on a mushroom.

He slowly looked at her.

“Who are you?”

“I'm Alice.”

The Caterpillar waited.

“At least, I think I am.”

“You think?”

“I've been so many different sizes today that I'm not completely sure.”

The Caterpillar nodded as though this were perfectly normal.

“One side will make you taller. The other side will make you shorter.”

“One side of what?”

“The mushroom.”

“Which side does which?”

But the Caterpillar was already crawling away.

Alice broke off two small pieces.

After several bites, one very long neck, and an argument with a pigeon who thought she was a serpent, Alice finally reached a comfortable size.

Soon she came to a crooked little house.

Nearby sat a large striped cat with an enormous grin.

“Hello,” said Alice.

The cat grinned.

“Could you tell me which way I should go?”

“That depends on where you want to go.”

“I don't really know.”

“Then any way will do.”

Alice frowned.
